These subjective-style Biology tests ask you to formulate an answer, explain a process and use the subject’s vocabulary in context. The collection spans cell structure, plant tissues and coordination, nutrition, respiration, human transport and excretion, the nervous system, sensory organs, disease topics and mixed review.

Use it for school Biology revision and to strengthen the explanation skills that support NEET and other competitive study. The tests are practice tools; check detailed facts against your current syllabus and trusted learning materials.

Make each attempt useful

Keep a small “explain again” list. After a test, note the concept where your response felt incomplete. Revisit just that concept, then test yourself again later without notes.

A practical weekly Biology writing cycle

Use this seven-day rhythm alongside your regular lessons and syllabus.

Days 1–2 · foundationsCell & tissue

Revise core terms; attempt two cell or tissue tests. Draw and label structures from memory where helpful.

Days 3–4 · processesPlants & life

Practise plant coordination, transport, nutrition and respiration. Write each process in ordered steps.

Days 5–6 · systemsHuman biology

Complete system, brain, nerve, sensory organ and disease tests. Review explanations that missed a function or reason.

Day 7 · retrieveMixed review

Attempt both mixed tests without notes, then set next week’s priorities from your error log.

How to improve a written answer

Small habits that make Biology revision more active.

Lead with the idea.
Start with a direct definition or main claim.
Show the relationship.
Explain the function, cause, comparison or sequence the question asks for.
Finish with accuracy.
Check terms, spelling, labels and whether every part was answered.
